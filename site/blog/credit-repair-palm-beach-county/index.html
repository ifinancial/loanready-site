Most people start looking into credit repair right after a bad moment: a declined application, a rate quote that made no sense, or a lender who said "come back when your score is higher." If that's you, you're in good company. A large share of the owners and families who walk into our office on Northlake Blvd got here exactly that way.

This guide explains what credit repair can and can't do, the errors we see most often on reports from Palm Beach County residents, how to protect yourself from the scams that give this industry a bad name, and how to approach repair if your real goal is a loan.

Why your credit report matters more than your score

Your score is a summary. Your report is the evidence behind it, and underwriters read the evidence. A 690 with a clean, consistent report and low card balances can get a better answer than a 710 with a recent collection, eleven inquiries and a maxed-out card.

Lenders also price in tiers. A mortgage lender uses your middle score of the three bureaus. An equipment lender might have a pricing break at 680 and another at 720. A bank reviewing a line of credit will look at every owner's personal report, not just the business. That's why repair work should be aimed at the specific loan you want, not at a number in an app.

What credit repair can legally do

The federal Fair Credit Reporting Act gives you the right to dispute information on your credit report that's inaccurate, incomplete or can't be verified. When you dispute, the credit bureau generally has 30 days to investigate (sometimes up to 45). If the furnisher can't verify the item, it must be corrected or deleted.

That's the whole legal foundation. Good credit repair is the careful, persistent use of those rights, plus coaching on the habits that move scores.

Just as important is what stays. Accurate negative information can legally remain on your report:

  • Late payments, collections and charge-offs: generally up to 7 years.
  • Chapter 7 bankruptcy: up to 10 years. Chapter 13 typically 7.
  • Hard inquiries: stay for 2 years and usually affect FICO scores for about 12 months.

Anyone who promises to remove accurate, timely information is either mistaken or not being straight with you.

The errors we find most often

After reviewing hundreds of reports from clients across North Palm Beach, Palm Beach Gardens, Jupiter, West Palm Beach and the Treasure Coast, the same problems come up again and again:

  • Duplicate collections. The original creditor and one or two collection agencies all report the same debt, so it hits your score multiple times.
  • Wrong balances and statuses. An account you paid off still shows a balance, or a settled account is reported as unpaid.
  • Outdated items. Debts that should have aged off are still there, sometimes "re-aged" by a debt buyer with a newer date.
  • Mixed files. Accounts or addresses belonging to someone with a similar name, often a relative. This is common with Jr./Sr. names.
  • Inquiries you didn't authorize. Especially after shopping for a car or responding to "pre-qualify" offers online.
  • Medical collections. The three bureaus stopped reporting paid medical collections and medical collections under $500, but we still find them.
  • Identity theft. Accounts you never opened. These call for a fraud report and a different process than a standard dispute.

Any one of these can drag a score down tens of points. Several together can be the difference between a bank approval and a decline.

What you can do yourself, for free

You don't need a company to start. Here's what anyone can do today:

  • Pull all three reports for free at AnnualCreditReport.com. Read every line, not just the summary.
  • Dispute clear errors directly with Equifax, Experian and TransUnion online. Keep copies and dates of everything.
  • Lower your card balances. Utilization, the share of your limits you're using, is one of the biggest score factors. Under 30% helps. Under 10% helps more.
  • Set up autopay for at least the minimum on every account. One new late payment can undo months of progress.
  • Stop applying. Every new hard inquiry and new account resets the clock a little.

Many people get real results this way. Clients come to us when they don't have the time, when their file is complicated, or when there's a loan with a deadline on the line.

How to spot a credit repair scam

Credit repair is regulated by the federal Credit Repair Organizations Act and, in Florida, by state law for credit service organizations. Use those rules as your checklist. Walk away if a company:

  • Charges before doing the work. Federal law prohibits credit repair companies from collecting fees before services are fully performed.
  • Guarantees a score or a deletion. No one controls how a bureau or creditor responds.
  • Offers a "new credit identity" or CPN. Using a credit privacy number in place of your Social Security number on a credit application can be a federal crime. It's not a loophole.
  • Tells you not to contact the bureaus yourself, or to dispute accurate information as inaccurate.
  • Won't give you a written contract that explains your rights, including your right to cancel within three business days.

In Florida, credit service organizations are also required to maintain a surety bond. Ask about it. A legitimate company won't mind.

How long credit repair takes

Honest answer: it depends on the file. A report with two clear errors can improve within one dispute cycle, about 30 to 45 days. A report with collections across all three bureaus, a mixed file and recent late payments may need several months of work, plus habit changes on your side.

What speeds things up is focus. If you know what loan you're going for, you can prioritize the items that lender will care about most and leave the rest for later. That's how our Express Repair program works when a client has a deal on the table.

Frequently asked questions

Is credit repair legal in Florida?

Yes. You have a federal right to dispute inaccurate, incomplete or unverifiable information. Credit repair companies in Florida must follow the federal Credit Repair Organizations Act and Florida's credit service organization law, including bonding requirements and no fees before services are performed.

Will disputing items lower my credit score?

No. Filing a dispute doesn't lower your score. If an item is corrected or deleted, your score may go up.

Can you remove a late payment that's accurate?

Not through a dispute, if it's accurate and timely. Sometimes a creditor will agree to a goodwill adjustment, especially for a single late payment with an otherwise good history, but that's their choice.
