Business Credit

How to build business credit (and stop using your personal credit for everything)

A step-by-step guide to building a business credit profile that lenders and vendors take seriously.

Most small business owners fund their companies on personal credit: personal cards, personal loans, personal guarantees on everything. It works until it doesn't. One big purchase or one tough quarter can drag your personal score down and block both your business and your family from good financing.

Business credit is a separate profile tied to your company, not your Social Security number. Building it takes time, but it gives you more financing options, better vendor terms and a cleaner line between your business and personal finances. Here's how to do it.

What business credit is

Business credit bureaus track how your company pays its bills. The three main ones are Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business and Equifax Business. Each scores differently:

  • Dun & Bradstreet PAYDEX: a 1–100 score based on payment timeliness. Around 80 means you pay on time, and paying early can push it higher.
  • Experian Intelliscore Plus: a 1–100 score that blends payment history, credit use, public records and company information.
  • Equifax business scores: include payment and risk scores based on trade and financial data.

Unlike personal credit, business credit reports are generally available to anyone who pays for them, including vendors, lenders, landlords and potential partners.

Step 1: Set up a real, consistent business foundation

Lenders and bureaus need to see a legitimate, established business. Start here:

  • Form an LLC or corporation and keep it active with the Florida Division of Corporations (Sunbiz)
  • Get an EIN from the IRS (it's free)
  • Open a business bank account and run all business money through it
  • Use a business address and phone number, and list them the same way everywhere
  • Get any required local business tax receipts and licenses
  • Build a simple website and business email on your own domain

Consistency matters. If your business name, address and phone are listed differently on Sunbiz, your bank, your website and your Google profile, bureaus may create split or incomplete files.

Step 2: Get your D-U-N-S number

Dun & Bradstreet assigns a free D-U-N-S number that anchors your business file with them. Many lenders, vendors and government programs use it. You can request one directly from Dun & Bradstreet at no cost. You don't need to buy their paid packages to get one.

Step 3: Open accounts that report to business bureaus

Business credit is built by paying accounts that report your history. Not every vendor reports, so ask before you open an account:

  • Vendor or trade accounts (net-30): suppliers that let you buy now and pay within 30 days, and that report to one or more business bureaus. Office supply, shipping, fuel and industry suppliers are common starting points.
  • A business credit card that reports to business bureaus. Many still require a personal guarantee, but the account history builds your business profile.
  • Equipment leases and loans often report as well.

Start with a few accounts and use them regularly for things you'd buy anyway. You don't need dozens.

Step 4: Pay early, not just on time

Payment history is the biggest factor in business credit. With PAYDEX in particular, paying on the due date earns a solid score, while paying early can earn a higher one. Set reminders or autopay, and keep your business card balances reasonable. High utilization can hurt business scores just as it hurts personal ones.

Step 5: Monitor and correct your business reports

Errors happen on business reports too: wrong addresses, outdated owners, accounts that belong to a similarly named company. Check your files with each bureau periodically and correct anything inaccurate. Make sure your company information matches what's on Sunbiz and your bank records.

How long it takes

With a solid foundation and three to five reporting accounts paid early, many businesses establish a meaningful profile within about six months to a year. A strong profile that unlocks larger unsecured lines usually takes longer, and lenders will still consider time in business and revenue.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Mixing personal and business spending. It weakens your business file and complicates your books and taxes.
  • Paying for "instant business credit" packages. Credit can't be bought. It's earned by paying real accounts over time.
  • Using a CPN or someone else's credit. Never. It can be illegal and can destroy your ability to borrow.
  • Ignoring personal credit. Most small business lenders still review the owner's personal credit, especially for larger loans. Build both.
  • Letting your Sunbiz annual report lapse. An inactive entity is a red flag to lenders and vendors.

A 90-day business credit starter plan

  • Days 1–15: confirm your LLC or corporation is active on Sunbiz, get your EIN, open a dedicated business bank account, and make your business name, address and phone identical everywhere.
  • Days 15–30: request your free D-U-N-S number, set up a business phone listing and a simple website, and check for existing files with Experian Business and Equifax Business.
  • Days 30–60: open two or three net-30 vendor accounts that report to business bureaus, and use them for purchases you already make.
  • Days 60–90: pay every invoice early, add a business credit card that reports to business bureaus, keep balances low, and pull your business reports to confirm the accounts are showing.

After 90 days, keep paying early, add accounts gradually, and review your reports every quarter. Steady, boring consistency is what builds a strong business credit profile.

Business credit and bankability

Business credit is one piece of a bankable footprint. Banks look at your business credit, your personal credit, your financial statements, your tax returns and your bank statements together. When all of them agree, you get more options and better terms, including, for strong profiles, 0% introductory-rate business credit options.

Frequently asked questions

Does business credit affect my personal credit?

Generally, accounts reported only to business bureaus don't appear on your personal report. But if you personally guarantee a debt and default, it can affect your personal credit. Many business cards also check your personal credit when you apply.

How do I check my business credit score?

You can request your business reports and scores from Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business and Equifax Business. Some offer limited free access, and full reports are usually paid.

Do I need an LLC to build business credit?

A registered LLC or corporation with its own EIN, bank account and consistent contact information is the standard foundation. Sole proprietors have a much harder time separating business credit from personal credit.
