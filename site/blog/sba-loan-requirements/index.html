SBA loan requirements in 2026: a plain-English checklist

Credit, cash flow, ownership, equity, documents, and the 2025 rule changes that caught a lot of owners off guard.

SBA loans are some of the best financing a small business can get: long terms, competitive rates, and the ability to fund things banks often won't touch on their own. They're also some of the hardest to qualify for, and the rules got stricter in 2025.

This checklist covers what SBA lenders look for today, in plain English. The SBA guarantees part of the loan, but a bank or other approved lender makes the actual decision, and many lenders add requirements of their own on top of the SBA's.

The two main SBA programs

  • SBA 7(a): the workhorse program, up to $5 million, for working capital, equipment, inventory, buying a business, refinancing certain debt and owner-occupied real estate. Terms run up to 10 years for most uses and up to 25 years for real estate.
  • SBA 504: long-term, fixed-rate financing for owner-occupied commercial real estate and major equipment, structured with a bank and a Certified Development Company.

There's also SBA Express, a faster version of 7(a) for smaller amounts, with lender-driven decisions.

What changed in 2025

The SBA's current rulebook for these programs, known as SOP 50 10 8, took effect June 1, 2025, along with related notices. Key changes owners should know:

  • Streamlined "7(a) small loans" are now capped at $350,000, and the minimum FICO small-business score for that streamlined processing rose to 165. Many lenders set their own minimums higher.
  • SBA loan proceeds can't be used to refinance merchant cash advances. If your plan was to use an SBA loan to clear your MCAs, you'll need a different first step.
  • Ownership: businesses must be 100% owned by U.S. citizens, U.S. nationals or lawful permanent residents.
  • Equity injection: startups and business acquisitions generally require at least 10% equity from the buyer. A seller note can count toward part of it only if it's on full standby.
  • Upfront guaranty fees are back after a period when they were waived for smaller loans.

Rules continue to be updated, so confirm current requirements with your lender before you apply.

Credit requirements

SBA lenders look at the personal credit of every owner with 20% or more of the business, and often the business credit profile as well. While the SBA's formal threshold applies to its streamlined small loans, in practice most lenders want to see personal scores comfortably in the upper 600s or higher, a clean recent payment history, and no recent bankruptcies, foreclosures or collections they can't explain.

Federal debt matters too. A delinquent federal obligation, including a past-due EIDL loan, can block an application, and lenders check government databases for it.

Cash flow and debt coverage

The core question is whether your business generates enough cash to make the new payment and all existing debt payments. Lenders calculate this from your tax returns, adding back items like depreciation and interest, and compare it to your total annual debt service. Many want 1.25x coverage or better.

This is where aggressive write-offs hurt. If your returns show minimal profit, your coverage may not work even if your bank account says otherwise. We explain the fix in write-offs vs. loan approval.

Other eligibility basics

  • For-profit business operating in the U.S., meeting SBA size standards for your industry.
  • "Credit elsewhere" test: the lender must determine you can't get comparable financing on reasonable terms without the SBA guarantee.
  • Personal guarantees from owners of 20% or more.
  • Eligible use of proceeds and an eligible industry. Passive real estate investment and some other business types are excluded.
  • Collateral: lenders take available collateral on larger loans, but a lack of collateral alone usually isn't a reason to decline.

The SBA document checklist

  • Business tax returns, typically for the last three years
  • Personal tax returns for each 20%+ owner
  • Year-to-date profit and loss statement and balance sheet
  • Business debt schedule
  • Personal financial statement (SBA Form 413) for each owner
  • SBA Form 1919 (borrower information)
  • Business licenses, formation documents and ownership breakdown
  • For acquisitions or startups: business plan, projections, purchase agreement and source of equity injection
  • For real estate: purchase contract, appraisal and environmental review as required

Common reasons SBA applications get declined

  • Insufficient cash flow on the tax returns. The most common reason, usually caused by heavy write-offs or a weak prior year.
  • Credit issues on one owner. A recent late payment, collection or high utilization for any 20%+ owner can stall the whole file.
  • Existing merchant cash advances. They signal cash stress, and SBA proceeds can't be used to pay them off.
  • Delinquent federal debt, including past-due EIDL loans or unresolved tax liens.
  • Books that don't match the returns, or interim financials that can't be produced quickly.
  • Missing or unclear equity injection for acquisitions and startups.
  • Ownership that doesn't meet the 100% citizenship or permanent residency rule.

Every one of these can be identified, and most can be fixed, before you apply. That's the whole point of getting loan ready first.

How long SBA approval takes

With a complete file and an experienced lender, SBA 7(a) loans often close in about 30 to 90 days. Real estate deals and acquisitions can take longer. The biggest delays come from missing documents, books that don't match tax returns, and credit surprises discovered mid-process. A file that's ready on day one moves faster at every step.

A realistic path to an SBA loan

For many owners we meet, especially those carrying merchant cash advances, the SBA isn't the first step. It's the destination. The path usually looks like this:

  • Months 1–3: fix the footprint. Credit repair, books caught up, bank statements cleaned up, a tax plan in place.
  • Months 3–6: move into lower-cost conventional lines of credit and term loans that retire expensive short-term debt.
  • Months 6–12: apply for SBA financing with a clean, bankable file.

One of our clients, DMLPA, followed this path: credit repaired, high-interest cards and MCA debt paid off, three business lines of credit opened, and now working on a 10-year SBA loan.

Frequently asked questions

What credit score do I need for an SBA loan?

The SBA's streamlined 7(a) small loans require a FICO small-business score of at least 165, and many lenders set higher minimums. For personal credit, most SBA lenders want scores in the upper 600s or higher.

Can I use an SBA loan to pay off a merchant cash advance?

Not under current rules. Since 2025, SBA loan proceeds can't be used to refinance merchant cash advances. Conventional financing is usually the first step, with SBA afterward.

How much down payment does an SBA loan require?

It depends on the use. Startups and business acquisitions generally require at least 10% equity injection. Real estate and other uses vary by lender and program.
