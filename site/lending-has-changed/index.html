Bank lending has changed. Here's how to get approved anyway.

Lenders can see more than you think

When you apply for business financing, the lender doesn't just look at your score. They see:

  • Every recent hard inquiry on your personal credit, and often your business credit too. They know who else you've applied with and how recently.
  • UCC filings. Most merchant cash advances file a UCC lien. It's public, and lenders check it.
  • Your bank statements. Daily and weekly debits to other funders, NSFs, negative-balance days, and deposit swings are all right there.
  • Shared industry data. In the short-term funding world, funders share information about who has funded whom and how those deals performed.

So "apply everywhere and see who says yes" is now the fastest way to get told no.

Strategic submission: where, when, and how

Where. Every lender has its own box: credit tiers, industries, time in business, revenue, and how much existing debt they'll tolerate. We match your file to lenders whose guidelines you already meet. One well-matched submission beats ten random ones.

When. Timing changes the picture. That means after a strong deposit month, after your tax return is filed and shows the right income, after disputed items have cleared, and before new inquiries pile up. A few weeks can turn a decline into an approval.

How. A complete, consistent file: credit report, bank statements, tax returns and financial statements that all tell the same story. Underwriters decline files with holes because holes look like risk.

MCAs are underwriting like banks used to

Merchant cash advances used to be the easy yes. That's changing. In our experience, many funders now want credit in the 620s and up, more documentation, cleaner bank statements, and stricter limits on stacking. If short-term money is getting harder to get, waiting until you're desperate is the most expensive plan there is.

SBA money can't pay off an MCA anymore

Under SBA rules in effect since 2025, SBA loan proceeds can't be used to refinance merchant cash advances. The SBA also raised the minimum small-business credit score for its streamlined 7(a) small loans (now capped at $350,000), restored its upfront guaranty fees, and requires businesses to be 100% owned by U.S. citizens, nationals or lawful permanent residents. Owners who planned to "just get an SBA loan" to clear their advances need a different first step: conventional financing first, SBA after.

Taxes vs. borrowing power

Lenders size your loan off documented income. Writing off everything saves on taxes, but it can make your business look like it can't afford a payment. Proper tax planning balances both. You keep more from Uncle Sam and still qualify for the money you need. See how our tax planning works →

What "bankable" actually means

  • A clean, strong credit profile, with inaccurate items disputed and utilization under control
  • Few recent inquiries and no unexplained new debt
  • Bank statements without NSFs, negative days or stacked daily payments
  • Current books that tie to your tax returns
  • Tax returns that show enough income to cover the new payment
  • A plan for which lender comes first, and which comes next
